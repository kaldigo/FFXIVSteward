# Steward

A collection-aware companion for the maintained AutoRetainer integration fork. Open `/steward`. Defaults to log-only review. Enabled features follow AutoRetainer once log-only is off and required evidence is ready; there is no separate master automation switch. Other AutoRetainer functions remain its responsibility.

## Agreed rules

- Both Glamaholic (nested and legacy configurations) and native glamour plates are references.
- Preserve one exact collection copy of referenced/curated items, level-one equipment, nonmarketable blues (except confirmed ordinary dungeon/normal-raid sources) and special-source equipment. Marketable blues receive no protection from the blue-rarity rule, although all other protection rules still apply. Marketability means whether the item can be traded on the marketboard, independently of available listings or cached prices. Dyes do not affect protection or duplicates. Equipped and gear-set equipment stays protected.
- Armoire-eligible items are directed to the armoire, including pieces restored from the dresser. For other items, an exact item in the dresser or armoire satisfies collection ownership; surplus copies may be disposed of under AutoRetainer's existing settings. Ordinary unreferenced dungeon/crafted equipment can be unwanted even if uncollected. Unknown acquisition sources stay held. Crafted items are recognized from the game Recipe sheet. Dungeon sources are indexed incrementally through Glamour Log’s existing GetItemsFromContent IPC; missing sources stay conservative and can be overridden in Rules.
- Exclusions override all handling, including market actions. Both Metallic Silver Dye (8743) and General-purpose Metallic Silver Dye (13116) are excluded once on initial setup, with IDs verified against installed English game data. You can remove either exclusion later; it will not be re-added. Quantity reserves and the explicitly configured experimental silver strategy have their own controls.
- Ordered sale rules reserve worthwhile surplus before disposal, with minimum unit/stack values and stack size. HQ/NQ comparison is configurable. Known own retainer listings, including Allagan Market listings on the current world, are excluded from competition. If an own listing is already cheapest, do not lower it.
- Missing, future or stale competitor prices hold marketable items rather than valuing them at zero.

## Setup and controls

Build both plugins with `./tools/build.ps1`. Install the fork as a replacement for upstream AutoRetainer, never alongside it; its identity and existing configuration are preserved. Install Steward separately. Repository releases are development previews with immutable archives and checksums. Native testing is still required.

Update both plugins together: Steward requires AutoRetainer inventory-policy protocol 6 and holds on an older bridge. Older safety-protocol migrations use log-only mode before any execution handlers are registered. Current installations keep their explicit log-only and feature settings. Open the dresser, native plate editor and armoire to establish collection/reference evidence. Cached observations never substitute for unavailable live data. Zoning or native data unloading can therefore pause actions until the required data is loaded again. Only complete native reads replace collections, including unlocked outfit pieces. Source inventory slots and glamour references are refreshed before permission/submission. Glamaholic, Market Buddy and Allagan Market files are imported read-only; failures are visible. Expected Market Buddy settings becoming unavailable hold market execution instead of silently using defaults. Universalis current-world sale summaries use the current-market endpoint with statistics-only fields (no listing/history payload), in batches of up to 20 items, with at most four requests in flight and four starts per second, including in log-only mode. The persistent summary cache defaults to two-hour background refresh, configurable to one or two hours in Sale rules. The sale-history window defaults to 28 days (configurable 7–90); the server considers up to 500 transactions per item. Reaching that cap makes velocity unknown and blocks disposal; prices remain provisional sale estimates. Average sale value and optional units-sold-per-day thresholds decide eligibility; no recent sales is unknown, never zero. Older known values can qualify slow sellers when the rule permits, but cannot authorize disposal. Actual posting still uses identified native competition and excludes owned retainers.

The searchable sidebar separates normal setup from developer diagnostics:

- **Overview:** feature controls, readiness, recovery, manual actions and current inventory decisions.
- **Sale rules:** ordered category/item rules, quality, price and sales-rate thresholds, stack sizes, pricing preferences, price-cache settings and the current retainer's listing preview. Rules, Pricing, Price data and Listing preview have separate tabs.
- **Glamour filters:** native plates, Glamaholic, the bundled Eorzea Collection selection, level-one/designed glamour gear, rarity and acquisition-source filters, with category scope for automatic filters.
- **Storage:** dresser reserve, protected retainer overflow, storage exclusions, non-equipment disposal categories, player alerts and opt-in dresser cleanup/armoire migration. Assign storage destinations only in AutoRetainer.
- **Item overrides:** exclusions, keep/collection overrides, quantity reserves, non-equipment disposal permissions and acquisition-source corrections.
- **Integrations:** AutoRetainer connection and guidance to the relevant settings pages.
- **Developer tools:** log-only mode, snapshot capture and diagnostic status/history. No normal setup settings or raw configuration editor live here.

Changing policy settings discards pending decisions while an already submitted action finishes acknowledgement. Existing settings migrate without enabling execution or changing the default protection policy. Filters combine: disabling one does not override another matching filter. Saved outfits, supplemental collection and pending collection holds are not narrowed by automatic-filter category selections. Unknown acquisition sources, gearsets, quantity reserves and live last-copy checks retain their safety gates. Normal dungeon and normal raid filters default off; alliance, savage and other high-end sources default on. Log-only defaults on. Stop current run cancels queued work; enabled features wait for a fresh authorised AutoRetainer run. Verified unsubmitted item failures are reported and skipped. A possibly submitted action with an unverified outcome, failed journal save or unsafe cleanup retains an inventory-review hold.

Preview and all companion-owned actions also query AutoRetainer's explicit protection through its existing read-only API. Protected items remain kept; unavailable protection holds. Enabling automatic execution requires a connected, reviewed character and a successful settings save. A failed save leaves execution off. Review recovery only clears local transaction state after the backend guards are saved successfully; saving failures restore in-memory holds.

Feature settings persist across plugin loads. Log-only defaults on for new installations; there is no master automation switch to re-enable. Verified unsubmitted item failures are reported and skipped, while uncertain submissions retain a review hold. All companion queue operations persist a pending record before their first native step and clear it only after acknowledgement and cleanup, including proven unsubmitted skips or cancellations. Possibly submitted interrupted work requires review on the character that owned it. AutoRetainer's vendor, desynthesis and armoire paths likewise submit once and wait for the expected source change before proceeding. Stop AutoRetainer's queued work and close pending dialogs before clearing a stopped run or detaching.

Steward migrates its existing configuration to `Steward/steward-state.json` inside Dalamud's plugin configuration directory; this synchronous private state becomes authoritative. The ordinary Dalamud configuration remains a compatibility mirror. The fork separately migrates its companion ownership/hold markers to `AutoRetainer/steward-policy.json`. Its usual configuration, identity and unrelated settings retain their existing handling. Ordinary AutoRetainer configuration imports cannot remove that private policy. Back up these private files with the plugin configurations; do not delete them to clear a stopped run. Damaged safety state holds startup instead of silently resetting protections.

AutoRetainer's retainer postprocess handoff opens inventory, withdraws eligible items through the fork, waits for source and destination evidence, then opens the market list and runs sequential listings/repricing. Current-retainer work can also be requested explicitly. Market Buddy's named lock is released on completion, failure, cancellation or unload. AutoRetainer postprocessing is released only for our own handoff; its bridge watches the lease and releases a stranded Steward handoff when the connection expires. Standalone market/dresser work use a separate owned control lease that pauses AutoRetainer's main loop while idle control is held and expires if Steward disappears. The general suppression flag is never overwritten. Losing ownership stops Steward. Retainer identity and source fingerprints are checked throughout. Unknown outcomes and partial planning failures immediately disable automatic execution and persist an inventory-review hold. Clearing the hold leaves automatic execution off. Menu timeouts and partial delegated-request failures also hold.

Armoire/Dresser/GC/Desynth/Vendor buttons request AutoRetainer's existing workflows. The companion dresser request leaves the dresser open for the native targeted adapter. Automatic mode asks the accompanying AutoRetainer fork to sweep reachable retainers for missing collection items and to open the dresser through its existing inn route. While the dresser is idle, it processes permitted deposits and cleanup through the same queue. Collection holds are saved before withdrawals. Missing repairs, prisms, native filter entries or storage access are reported without discarding items. AutoRetainer feature settings and explicit protections still apply. These requests do not rewrite existing disposal lists. Every armoire-eligible item gets a collection route before sale/disposal. Clean open dresser restores eligible loose items only after evaluating protection with that exact dresser copy omitted, even when live data is refreshed during the check. Partial-outfit restoration selects only the permitted unlocked piece and checks the destination inventory. Targeted loose-item deposits require the matching native source and item-specific confirmation. Bulk Glamour Log outfit conversion is held for managed characters because its IPC cannot revalidate each item at submission; unsupported conversion dialogs also stop the queue. Managed discard, including delayed discard confirmations, is disabled. GC delivery requires an owned, unexpired pending item with unchanged inventory, matching reward text and fresh protection permission at confirmation. Native acceptance remains required.

History distinguishes policy permission from actual execution. AutoRetainer owns its disposal results; Steward records confirmed transfers/listings/restorations and request failures. The session view is bounded; `history.jsonl` remains local to Steward's configuration directory. On unload the character's claim remains, so unavailable policy cannot accidentally resume disposal. Integration > Detach explicitly restores original AutoRetainer rules.

GC commit buttons are one-shot while awaiting acknowledgement. Backend uncertainty persists a review hold in AutoRetainer as well as Steward; clearing it requires resolving pending confirmations and using the existing review-clear button. Owned standalone control/review holds reuse AutoRetainer's existing YesAlready/TextAdvance pause requests. Cancelling an owned pending dresser confirmation selects No. Independent third-party UI automation and native pause/confirmation behaviour still require in-game acceptance.

## Log-only verification before execution

**Developer tools → Log only — do not change any items** defaults on, including when upgrading settings that lack this field. It records proposed listings/prices, disposal and, when the cleanup toggle is enabled, dresser restoration/armoire migration without submitting any managed item action. All managed disposal, listing, retrieval and storage permissions remain denied even if an automatic flag is present. Switching modes stops queued work and leaves automatic execution off; already submitted game actions require the existing cancellation/review handling.

Use **Write review snapshot now** for a complete new snapshot, or leave the mode enabled for scans every five seconds. Changes are written to `review.jsonl` beside the private Steward settings, with readable entries also in History. Unchanged observations are suppressed. Entries identify the character, item ID, quantity, source/container/slot, proposed action and reason. Cached retainer observations are explicitly labelled and restricted to identified current-character retainers; no retainer menus are opened for logging. Pending collection holds and readiness gaps are logged so zero proposals cannot be mistaken for complete verified coverage. No log write failure permits execution.

Only turn log-only off after reviewing the output, then separately enable automatic execution. The dresser-cleanup toggle remains independently opt-in. This mode does not disable player-controlled game actions, unrelated plugins or AutoRetainer's non-policy functions such as venture collection.

## Automatic listing and market preview

Normal AutoRetainer retainer postprocessing opens the current retainer inventory/market and runs the companion market queue. New listings require an enabled matching sale rule, readable Market Buddy preferences when imported, and fresh valid competition. AutoRetainer retains coordination and its existing inventory/disposal workflows. Universalis supplies cached average values and optional daily-sales thresholds; native market comparisons still gate submissions. An enabled velocity threshold holds unknown evidence rather than allowing the native planner to bypass the rule.

The queue reprices existing eligible listings first. It then reads actual market occupancy and selects a live eligible source stack. Each listing uses the sale-rule/Market Buddy stack caps and actual unit/total thresholds. After confirmed source removal and a matching new market slot, it re-reads the remainder and available slots, repeatedly splitting the same source stack until exhausted or the market is full. A below-floor remainder, missing comparison or rejected source does not reserve a slot; other candidates are tried. Skipped sources are reconsidered on a later pass. Additional stacks match a known lowest own listing instead of repeatedly undercutting it. Bound/collectable/glamoured instances are not submitted as new listings.

**Sale rules → Current retainer preview** shows proposed split quantities, unit/total prices and repricing proposals. Opening the retainer selling list automatically refreshes the preview and captures it in log-only mode, even with Steward's window closed; no button click is needed. Background summaries and observed comparisons provide estimates, not permission to submit. Detailed holds and price provenance remain in review logs. Actual execution rechecks competition, owned listings, quantities, protections and capacity before posting. See [market listing details](docs/market-listing.md).

## Dresser capacity, overflow and cleanup

See [storage policy and acceptance checks](docs/storage-policy.md). The empty-slot slider defaults to 20. Protected overflow is deposited with available retainers using AutoRetainer, with persistent per-character protection until collection is confirmed. Chat, notification and history alerts report overflow, destinations and unresolved space shortages. Automatic retainer visits need an accessible summoning bell; otherwise the items stay protected and the player is alerted.

Dresser cleanup is opt-in and starts disabled. Its preview checks all rules. Normal dungeon/normal-mode raid blues can be restored if nothing else protects them; Alliance, Savage, Coil and other high-end sources remain protected. Eligible dresser pieces are restored under a durable armoire-transfer hold and passed to AutoRetainer's existing armoire workflow. Retainer overflow does not count as a collected copy and is not withdrawn while the dresser remains at its reserve.

## Curated Eorzea Collection list

The bundled list uses a job-balanced Eorzea Collection survey, an official usage-census cross-check and exact per-item all-time use counts. Frequency leads; likes from the most-loved results page support the ranking. Exact game IDs are verified against installed English game data. Level-one equipment, nonmarketable blues outside the ordinary-duty exception and identified special rewards are covered by other rules. See [the full list, method and slot/job coverage](docs/common-glamour.md) and [saved evidence](docs/data/common-glamour-evidence.json). The survey is bounded and cannot infer every personal preference. **Glamour filters → Eorzea Collection** enables the internal bundled selection; no file link/import control is exposed. It supplements the other default protection rules rather than duplicating every popular item already covered by them. Maintainers update it for major patches using the preserved [curation tooling and procedure](tools/ec-curation/README.md), game-data exporter, frozen evidence and deterministic builder.

## Current verification limits

Local builds and automated rules/import/pricing/cancellation checks pass. No FFXIV in-game tests have been performed. Market prices require complete responses matching the native item/request, followed by readback of both the native agent and visible price/quantity fields and a final threshold/source check. Network callbacks only enqueue managed snapshots; native reads run on the framework thread. Confirmation parent/host links must match the expected source window. Unknown identities/layouts hold. Callback behaviour, ownership fields, request IDs/counts, retainer menus, withdrawals and acknowledgements still require native acceptance. Cross-zone workflows may remain held when native collection data is cleared; a safe refresh path for every location has not been verified.

Glamour Log’s bulk outfit IPC has no per-item submission callback, so it is blocked for managed characters. Acquisition indexing depends on source metadata; unknown equipment sources stay held. Common-glamour discovery is sampled and cannot infer every personal keep preference. Allagan Tools inventories are cached previews, never execution authority. Automatic travel/storage/disposal schedules and configured disposal lists remain owned by AutoRetainer. The safeguards cover connected, managed characters; an upstream installation or a deliberately detached character retains its original AutoRetainer behaviour. See [the safety review](docs/safety-review.md) for fixes, automated evidence and the native acceptance boundary.

Sale rules use grouped, searchable category checkbox panels with the same game icons as AutoRetainer storage. Weapons, tools, armour, accessories, consumables, materials and other items have separate sections in wrapping icon rows, with hover names and selection summaries. Each sale rule has its own visible summary card, reorder controls and expandable details. New rules have no Gear/Other selector; older rules retain their existing broad restriction until the user explicitly removes it. Give gear and materials different prices/stack sizes, and move specific rules above catch-all rules. Global category exclusions leave whole item types untouched; per-rule exclusions affect only that rule. Category sale rules never override collection protection or permit material disposal. Unpriced Universalis items are logged individually and no longer block the rest of a batch.

The supplemental collection list (Collect glamour item) sends one missing equipment copy to collection; Keep item remains a physical-inventory override. Per-item quantity reserves keep live inventory quantities outside collection. Offline retainer caches are not proof of a surviving reserve. Split listings subtract only removable quantities, and whole-stack disposal is held when any part is reserved.

The review journal now includes collection destinations, retainer overflow, sale eligibility and its average/velocity evidence, individual holds, exclusions and keep reasons. A collection classification is not a claim that a withdrawal or deposit has occurred. AutoRetainer disposal selection is checked before retrieving a disposal candidate; Steward does not rewrite its lists or choose speculative desynthesis returns.

The accompanying fork adds optional Collect and DisposalSelected integration operations without changing AutoRetainer identity or protocol-6 safety semantics. On an older fork, standalone collection sweeps cannot run and disposal retrieval stays held. Native acceptance of the new sweep/travel paths remains required before publishing. See docs/feature-audit.txt for the feature-by-feature review.

Gateway-timeout recovery: failed batches shrink and their IDs are deferred while other prices load. HTTP 429 and Retry-After pause the whole source; repeated independent errors also back off. Successful responses and persisted values survive sibling failures. These limits leave capacity for other Universalis-using plugins.

Retainer inventories, dresser and armoire observations persist per character in inventory-observations.json without timed expiry. Complete native observations replace them, including empty inventories; Allagan Tools can seed missing retainer snapshots. These observations support previews and price prefetching across restarts. Cached collection copies are separately labelled in logs and can only preserve items: they never establish live ownership, release collection holds, supply quantity reserves, or authorize disposal/cleanup. Open the relevant storage to refresh its live evidence.

Price prefetch starts on the first framework refresh with a loaded character and current world, even with the Steward window closed and Automatic off. Saved retainer inventories supply candidates without opening storage. Missing/expired prices refresh in the background; still-valid prices are used immediately. Character/world changes rebuild the candidate list. First-time installations seed observations from Allagan Tools or native storage reads.

Allagan retainer imports preserve their sorted 35-slot bag positions in a separate preview namespace. They are never interpreted as native slots; a live retainer read replaces the snapshot. Review/history serialization and batched file writes run through one ordered background writer. Manual snapshot buttons schedule a framework review, errors remain visible, and unloading drains queued writes. All game reads, protection decisions and submission checks remain on the game thread.

Reference readiness is rebuilt after every native inventory refresh, independently of slower market-cache imports. Truly unloaded native plate data or unreadable selected outfits still hold actions. Opening a retainer selling list in log-only mode immediately requests a review snapshot and continues refreshing automatically.

## Sales activity and listing windows

Each sale rule accepts a **minimum average units sold per day**, including fractions: 0.1 is about one unit per ten days. This is market-wide demand, not your own sales count. An enabled threshold requires known recent sales-rate evidence even if Universalis fetching is disabled; native asking prices cannot substitute for it.

Optional **Rotate long-unsold listings** sets a per-rule maximum listing window (60 days when enabled). Steward starts tracking a listing when first observed; its earlier age is unknown. Repricing does not restart that clock; changed quantities/items and confirmed removal do. The clock records observed continuity, so an identical item manually removed and replaced between observations cannot be distinguished. Tracking survives restart and is scoped by character, world and retainer.

Rotation requires a full market, at least two empty player inventory slots and a different eligible item waiting in the current retainer/player inventory. At most one listing is rotated per market pass. Steward first checks the replacement against fresh native competition, then records its queue position before returning it to inventory. It verifies source removal and the inventory quantity increase before filling the slot. Waiting items retain their queue position; the returned item goes behind items already waiting and is relisted when it is eligible and a slot becomes available. All existing price, stack, protection and own-listing safeguards still apply. Returned items are assessed by the same collection, sale and disposal rules as other inventory.

Returns do not grant special protection. Changing rules can make returned items eligible for AutoRetainer disposal, subject to the ordinary collection, reserve and non-equipment permission safeguards. Safety revision 8 removes legacy relisting reserves and restores log-only on upgrade; uncertain-operation and collection holds are unchanged.

Log-only records listing ages, configured windows, proposed rotations and rule-based withdrawals without withdrawing anything. The new native withdrawal/context-menu interaction has not been exercised in FFXIV; its identity, menu ownership and destination checks may hold on an unsupported UI layout.

Sale automation has a global Retainers selection. Each rule can optionally narrow it; a global exclusion cannot be overridden. Excluded retainers are still recognised as yours for undercut protection. Existing listings on enabled retainers are checked against current rule scope, average value, demand, current listing floors and stack limits. Known failures are returned; missing evidence holds them. Log-only records WouldReturnListing without acting.

Combined pricing selects the lowest positive listing across both qualities, independently of historical sale averages. Separate pricing selects only the item quality. Summaries remain estimates without seller identities.

The sidebar uses Steward’s bundled icon, centred above navigation, following the layout reference in [Wrath’s configuration window](https://github.com/PunishXIV/WrathCombo/blob/main/WrathCombo/Window/ConfigWindow.cs). No Wrath code or artwork is included.

At native listing/repricing time, ignore 1–3 cheapest competitor listings only if every skipped price is at most 25% of the next price and the next ten distinct listings are within twice that next price. Count listings, not stack quantities; own listings cannot supply the ten supporting entries and still prevent self-undercutting. Four or more cheap outliers, weak support, or unidentified/duplicate rows do not trigger the safeguard. Average-value sale/disposal decisions and summary-only previews are unchanged. Native-comparison previews and execution history explain any ignored outliers.

Feature controls on Overview independently enable new listings, repricing, rule-based listing withdrawals, window rotation, dresser collection, armoire storage, overflow storage and AutoRetainer disposal. Dresser cleanup retains its separate opt-in switch. Relevant switches also appear on Sale rules, Glamour filters and Storage. Rotation requires new listings enabled; collection/overflow routes are independent of sale-retainer exclusions. Disabled actions are recorded as FeatureDisabled rather than proposed actions in review logs. Turning off a feature preserves its rules and protection evidence: disabled collection never releases a protected last copy, and disabling sales does not turn sale-reserved items into disposal candidates. Disposal off denies managed vendor/desynthesis/GC permissions while Steward remains connected. Changes pause execution and preserve settings for later re-enablement.

Native glamour-plate references are saved per character after a successful in-game observation and reused on later starts without a TTL. The UI and review evidence distinguish saved references from live plate data. Any later live plate read replaces the cache, including an empty set. Missing/corrupt/unobserved snapshots remain unavailable. This cache contains outfit references, not inventory ownership; it never establishes dresser/armoire copy counts. If plates are changed while Steward is absent or on another computer, open the editor once to refresh the saved references.

Category selectors follow the marketboard grouping: Main Arm/Off Arm (weapons and tools), Armor (including shields and accessories), Items, and Housing. Hand variants share one highlighted icon button; partially selected legacy pairs have an amber border without changing their stored scope. Group Select all/Clear all act on the entire group even during search. Existing empty-selection defaults are unchanged and described above each selector.
